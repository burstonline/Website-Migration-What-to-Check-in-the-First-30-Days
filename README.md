# Website-Migration-What-to-Check-in-the-First-30-Days
Review what needs monitoring in the first 30 days after a website migration, including customer journeys, tracking, search visibility, performance and integrations.


The launch checklist is finished and the new website is online. Just as the team begins to relax, a customer reports a failed form or a sales report shows an unexplained decline. The first month is a chance to establish whether the site works for customers and for the people operating the business. Website performance covers more than the speed of the homepage. Over the first 30 days, examine the important journeys, measurements and system connections, then use the findings to build a clear list of repairs.

**Check the Most Important Customer Journeys First**

Start with the actions that give the site its purpose. People need to find a service, send an enquiry or finish the relevant purchase journey. Test these routes on the live website. A page that opens correctly does not establish that every step of the experience works.
Follow the route from the entry page through to the completed action.
When a step fails, record the page and device along with the exact point of failure. 'Mobile form stops after Submit' gives the team a more useful starting point than 'the new site is broken'. Maintain a short priority list in the first days, and test each repaired route again before marking the issue closed.

**Make Sure the Measurements Remain Meaningful**

A well-presented report can still give a misleading account. Review which website actions are recorded as enquiries or purchases, and compare them with the business records your team has. An unexplained discrepancy needs investigation. It does not immediately establish that the migration altered demand.
Use comparable information before assessing the outcome.
Apply consistent definitions and reporting periods. If the previous report measured a different action, document that difference before treating it as a baseline. Keep a simple review log of changes, checks performed and numbers that remain uncertain. Clear measurement makes website performance easier to discuss without disputes over totals that cannot be compared.

**Review Search Visibility and the Pages Visitors Reach**

Give search visibility a separate review. Our website treats search monitoring, indexing and website health as related concerns. Following migration, check which pages visitors reach from search and whether those pages still help them find a useful next step.
Focus on important service, category and product pages as well as the homepage. Record which pages previously generated useful enquiries or sales. Investigate unexpected changes with the people responsible for technical visibility, without assuming that one fluctuating figure explains the whole situation.
Throughout the first month, combine search reporting with direct checks of the pages themselves. Can visitors understand the offer and take the next step? Visibility and usability reviews answer different questions. Considering them together provides a clearer basis for deciding where attention is needed.

**Test Speed and Usability on Actual Devices**

Monitor performance in terms of the visitor's experience too. Server response figures or desktop checks alone cannot show everything involved in browsing on a phone. Test relevant pages, forms and purchase steps, noting delays and interactions that are difficult to complete.
Try the mobile navigation and complete the forms.
Observe how important pages load.
Describe visible errors in enough detail for someone to reproduce them.
Combine these observations with performance reports when choosing the next repair. Record the work instead of making several unrelated changes without documentation. Repeating the journey should let you identify what has improved and what still gets in the way. A website that people can use is the objective; a dashboard that looks reassuring is not enough.

**Trace Orders and Enquiries Through Other Systems**

Migration may affect systems beyond the public website. Our services link storefronts to business systems, including customer management, inventory and reporting. A successful-looking website is only one stage in that chain. Verify that the information expected from it reaches its next destination.
Identify the handoffs required in your setup. An enquiry might need to populate a customer record, while an order might need to reach an operating system. Have the responsible team check the full route and document gaps, rather than testing each application separately.
At the end of the month, summarize resolved problems and remaining risks, along with the next improvements. Give every outstanding item an owner. This turns the first 30 days into a useful period of operational review rather than a pile of unrelated screenshots, and provides a defined starting point for ongoing maintenance.

**Common Questions**

**Should Website Checks Begin Only After 30 Days?**
No. Check essential journeys as soon as the website goes live, then continue reviewing measurements and connected systems during the month. Thirty days provides a framework for organizing the work, not a reason to postpone investigating a failure.

**Does Fewer Reported Enquiries Mean the Migration Failed?**
Not necessarily. Examine the enquiry route, measurement definitions and business records before identifying a cause. Distinguish a genuine reduction in enquiries from a problem in reporting.

**What Should Come Out of the First-Month Review?**
Produce a clear record of what works, what has been repaired and what still needs attention. Each open issue needs enough information for investigation and an owner to see it through.

**Turning the First Month's Checks Into a Plan**
Checks made during the first month should give the team an evidence-based view of the migrated site. Begin with customer journeys and validate the measurements. Review search visibility and test the systems connected behind the website. Record each problem and assign responsibility, then verify the repair and keep its result. Website performance is easier to improve when the team shares the same evidence. By day 30, aim for a dependable account of how the new site serves customers and a practical plan to address the remaining issues.
